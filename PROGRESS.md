# Progress

Last updated: 2026-08-31

## Current State

The pipeline is past first-run and running against real household data (2025-01 through 2026-08). Three sources, all plain CSV: Simplifi (manual export), `store-receipt-extract` (homegrown Chrome extension, Target + Costco), Amazon Order History Reporter (browser extension). See `README.md` for the full setup and monthly workflow, and `docs/adding-a-source.md` for wiring in a new source (Walmart is the anticipated next one, not yet built).

Google Cloud project: `spending-pipeline`. Auth is personal `gcloud`/Application Default Credentials, not a service account — no Firestore, no saved-mapping persistence layer. `config/merchant_rules.yaml` is the entire categorization mechanism.

BigQuery (`spending-pipeline.finance_pipeline`) is the durable, queryable store. Nine views, now checked into `sql/views/*.sql` with `sql/views/deploy.sh` to (re)apply them in dependency order: `v_item_level_monthly_category`, `v_unmatched_simplifi_spend` (shared row-level base for the three views below — single source of truth for the "exclude already-matched, exclude administrative spending_class" filter), `v_simplifi_monthly_category`, `v_blended_monthly_category`, `v_monthly_category_totals` (by household category, monthly), `v_monthly_spending_class_totals`/`v_monthly_spending_class_pivot` (by budget type, monthly), `v_spend_detail` (row-level, not aggregated — connect directly in Google Sheets to eyeball categorization by sorting on amount), and `v_category_mapping` (grouped rule/category/merchant lookup with `$` totals for cross-checking against `merchant_rules.yaml`). `retailer` is populated from `merchant_normalized` everywhere, including Simplifi-only rows, for full drill-down during validation. Two skills in `.claude/skills/`: `categorization-quality` (read-only audit, dollar-ranked punch list) and `unknown-category-review` (LLM-assisted review of `Unknown_Review` items that writes confirmed fixes straight into `merchant_rules.yaml`).

**Simplifi raw exports must be replaced, not accumulated** — `data/raw/simplifi/` should hold exactly one export file. Leaving an old one in place alongside a new one double-loads every transaction in the overlapping date range (hit this for real on 2026-08-31: an old 08-19 export left next to a new 08-31 one doubled the blended total from ~$393k to ~$783k before it was caught and fixed). README already documented this; there's no code guard against it.

## Next Steps

**Reconciliation match-quality audit — picking this up next session.** Different axis from categorization: `matched_simplifi_transaction_id` pairs an item-level order with a Simplifi transaction using a date window (±5 days, Amazon gets an extended ±10-day fallback for orders ≥$10) plus amount tolerance ($0.03 accounting / $0.05 matching) — fuzzy enough that a close-but-wrong transaction could plausibly get matched instead of the right one, especially two same-week same-amount orders from the same retailer. Nothing currently checks whether a *correct* match is actually correct, only whether *some* match happened within tolerance.

Sketch for a `reconciliation-quality` skill (sibling to `categorization-quality`, not a merge — reads `reconciliation_detail.csv` and the `matched_simplifi_transaction_id` linkage rather than the category files):
- Flag matches sitting near the tolerance edge (`item_vs_simplifi_difference` close to $0.03/$0.05) rather than exact — those are the ones most likely to be a coincidental match, not a real one.
- Flag the classic swap signature: two items/transactions in the same date window where each is individually within tolerance of the *other's* counterpart, not just its own.
- For any retailer/week with more than one matched order, verify no other unmatched or differently-matched transaction in the same window would have been an equally good or better fit.
- Same reporting discipline as the other two skills: dollar-ranked findings, never auto-fixes reconciliation config, human confirms before anything changes.

## Recent Work (2026-08-31)

Audited what actually lands in BigQuery, driven by the user's stated cadence (full 2-year Simplifi reload, twice a month) and goal (trust the blended-category totals enough to actually budget off them). Found and fixed four real issues, all now live:

- **Simplifi's household-category crosswalk was two hand-duplicated, incomplete `CASE` statements embedded directly in `v_simplifi_monthly_category` and `v_spend_detail`**, reading the *raw* `simplifi_category` instead of the already-cleaned `simplifi_category_mapped` column. Silent `ELSE` passthrough meant anything not in the hardcoded list became its own ad hoc category — caught a real leak where "02 Costco" (a Simplifi category, cleaned by `config/simplifi_category_mapping.yaml` to "Household Operations") never reached that cleanup and sat as a standalone `Costco` bucket (~$4.6k) instead of folding into `Household`. Fixed by moving the crosswalk into Python (`normalize.py::blended_category_for_simplifi`, driven by new `config/simplifi_blended_category_mapping.yaml`), stored as a `blended_category` column on `transactions`, single-sourced and unit-tested. Design confirmed with the user: categories *without* an explicit rename entry stay exactly as Simplifi labels them (`Mortgage and Escrow` stays `Mortgage and Escrow`) — only the categories with a real item-level equivalent (groceries, restaurants, alcohol, health, home improvement, gas/auto, travel, gifts, entertainment) get renamed so the two sides roll up together.
- **`items_needing_review` and four sibling tables (`reconciliation_summary`, `reconciliation_detail`, `unmatched_simplifi_transactions`, `unmatched_retail_orders`, `category_rule_coverage`) were pure `WRITE_APPEND`**, so every twice-monthly full-history rerun re-appended the entire dataset on top of the last one. After 7 historical runs, `items_needing_review` had ballooned to 10,145 rows for only 1,456 distinct items; `unmatched_simplifi_transactions` was at 20,853 rows. Fixed: `BigQueryAnalyticsStore.write_table` now uses `WRITE_TRUNCATE` (matches the actual workflow — always a full reload, never incremental, so replace-in-place is correct and simpler than adding merge keys). One clean rerun brought `items_needing_review` back to 1,449 rows / 1,449 distinct items / 1 run.
- **`retailer` was hardcoded `NULL` on every Simplifi-sourced row** in all 3 monthly-rollup views and `v_spend_detail`. By design for the "no single retailer on a non-itemized card transaction" case, but during active validation the user needs to drill into merchant-level detail everywhere. Switched to `merchant_normalized` (already computed, already stored) so a Costco gas swipe, an unitemized Target run, or a Comcast bill all show up under their own retailer/merchant instead of one big NULL bucket. Confirmed: `NULL` retailer count in `v_blended_monthly_category` went from 319 rows to 0, with total spend unchanged (~$392.8k) — no rows lost or double-counted.
- **Added `v_category_mapping`**: grouped from `retail_items` by `category_rule_id`/category/merchant with count and `$` total, sorted by `$` desc — a lookup surface to spot a rule sending real money to the wrong category and go fix it in `merchant_rules.yaml` (immediately surfaced the $695.74 shed at the top, same one `v_spend_detail` caught earlier).
- All 8 view definitions are now checked into `sql/views/*.sql` with `sql/views/deploy.sh` — previously they existed only inside BigQuery itself (created via console/`bq` per the 2026-08-20 session), with no git history or reproducibility.
- **Found but did not fix**: `BigQueryAnalyticsStore._merge_dataframe`'s `MERGE` statement fails outright if the target table is missing a column the incoming DataFrame has (hit this immediately — had to manually `ALTER TABLE transactions ADD COLUMN blended_category STRING` before the reload would run). Worth adding schema-evolution handling if the schema keeps changing at this pace.

A `/code-review` pass on the above caught three more real issues, all fixed and reloaded:
- **Case-mismatch bug that reintroduced the exact leak just fixed**: `config/simplifi_blended_category_mapping.yaml`'s key `"Groceries and foods"` (lowercase f, matching real Simplifi exports) silently missed `simplifi_category_mapping.yaml`'s own output `"02 Groceries and Foods"` (capital F, produced when a raw category is literally `"Groceries"`) — an exact-match dict lookup, so the rename never fired for that spelling and it would've leaked through as a near-duplicate `Groceries and Foods` bucket. Fixed by making `blended_category_for_simplifi()`'s lookup case-insensitive. Added tests that actually exercise the mapped-through path (the original tests only checked the passthrough case).
- **`run-month --bigquery-project` was newly destructive**: it forwarded into the same `write_table()`/`WRITE_TRUNCATE` path as `run-period`, so running a single month against BigQuery would have replaced the full-history snapshot tables with just that one month. README already said `run-month` doesn't touch BigQuery; removed the option entirely so the code matches.
- **The "matched transactions" CTE + administrative-spending_class exclusion filter was copy-pasted across `v_simplifi_monthly_category`, `v_monthly_spending_class_totals`, and `v_spend_detail`** — the same duplication pattern that caused the original category-crosswalk drift. Extracted into a shared `v_unmatched_simplifi_spend` view; the three consumers now just `SELECT` from it.
- Also cached `load_yaml()` (`functools.lru_cache`) since `blended_category_for_simplifi` added a third per-row YAML re-read on top of two pre-existing ones, all reading files that never change mid-run.

**Also hit and fixed for real**: rerunning after the code-review fixes, the blended total jumped from ~$393k to ~$783k. Cause: the user dropped a new `Simplifi - Transactions_20260831.csv` into `data/raw/simplifi/` without removing the old `_20260819.csv` — exactly the double-load failure mode README already warns about (no stable Simplifi transaction ID, so the loader can't tell the same transaction apart across two overlapping full-history exports). Removed the stale file; `TRUNCATE`d `transactions`/`retail_items` (confirmed with the user first — the existing MERGE-upsert only adds/updates, it doesn't delete rows a bad prior run introduced) and reran clean. Final verified state: $400,741.63 total (in line with $392.8k plus 12 days of new activity), `items_needing_review` at 1,590 rows / 1,590 distinct items / 1 run, no orphan category buckets.

## Recent Work (2026-08-19 through 2026-08-20)

- Got Target, Costco, and Amazon item-level data flowing end to end alongside Simplifi; fixed a Target gift-card overcounting bug and an Amazon Google-Sheets footer-row crash.
- Closed most of the `Unknown_Review` gap by adding `category_prefix_rules` that key off each retailer's own category taxonomy (Amazon's product categories, Costco's `category_label`) — this did more than keyword rules alone.
- Found and fixed a real money-hiding bug in `spending_class_for_category()`: unmapped Simplifi categories defaulted to `Excluded` rather than `Review`, silently dropping ~$4,300 of real spend (daycare activities, ski passes, car repairs) from every total. Also caught and correctly excluded a $336K mortgage refinance transaction that the same fix would otherwise have swept into "spending."
- Stood up BigQuery from scratch (`gcloud` install, auth, billing, dataset, the 4 views above). Along the way found and fixed a real bug in `BigQueryAnalyticsStore._json_ready`: pandas' newer string dtype silently reverts a mapped-in `None` back to `NaN` on column reassignment, which BigQuery's JSON parser rejects outright. Never caught before because this was the first real BigQuery run ever attempted on this codebase.
- Removed OrderPro (all 4 retailer folders) and the Firestore-backed saved-mapping/state-persistence layer entirely — neither was in real use. Deleted ~4 loader modules, `mappings.py`, `storage/firestore_store.py`, `storage/memory_store.py`, and every CLI command/flag tied to them (`save-mapping`, `export-mappings`, `import-reviewed-mappings`, `accept/reject-mapping-candidate`, `--firestore-project`, `--mapping-csv`, `--persist-record-state`, `--queue-mapping-candidates`). Test suite went from 88 to 46 tests, all still real coverage of what's actually used.
- Redesigned the spending-class taxonomy: renamed `One-Time Project` to `Sinking` (better matches a household that does frequent DIY/travel/repairs — none of it is actually one-time) and split `Auto & Transport` so routine gas stays `Variable Required` while `Service & Parts`/`Registration` route to `Sinking`. Also gave `Ski Passes`/`Education`/`Education:Tuition` a real home in `Sinking` instead of the generic `Review` default. Added `v_monthly_spending_class_totals`/`v_monthly_spending_class_pivot` BigQuery views. Sinking now runs ~$3,400/month across the full history — a real, previously-invisible budget line.
- Built `v_spend_detail` (row-level BigQuery view, meant for Google Sheets) and the `categorization-quality` skill for auditing both categorization layers. The view immediately caught two real bugs: a Costco `retailer_fallback` defaulting unlabeled general merchandise to `Groceries` ($1,276.59, incl. a $695.74 shed), and `kw:grocery`'s `"apple"` keyword catching Apple-brand electronics (~$429) instead of just the fruit before `kw:electronics` got a chance. Both fixed. `Unknown_Review` rose from $938.85 to $2,215.44 as a direct, honest result — these items now surface for review instead of guessing confidently wrong.
- Built the `unknown-category-review` skill and ran it against that $2,215.44 backlog. Found the fixes were mostly *pattern* gaps, not one-offs — missing keywords (`shorts`/`vest` never in `kw:clothing`, `serum`/`sunscreen` never in `kw:health`, `shed`/`planter`/`weedclear` never in `kw:home_improvement`, `banana` singular but not plural in `kw:grocery`, etc.), so a handful of keyword-list additions cleared far more than their individual dollar amounts suggested. `Unknown_Review` dropped to $297.55 (164 rows). Also added `v_monthly_category_totals`/`v_monthly_spending_class_totals`/`v_monthly_spending_class_pivot`'s missing `retailer` column (dropped during aggregation, now preserved).
